## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment**: Focus is genuinely authored. The pass ticket with its 34pt tabular "4d", the heat-only colour, hollow punch marks per deferral, "Put off 3 times" and the strike-then-slide exit could not be dropped into another to-do app unchanged. It turns the product's one idea (put-off work comes back hotter) into visual grammar. Ink-on-paper chrome works in both themes, and the dark "pass after close" steel is a clear value step (ground #1D2124, tickets #2E3439), not near-black.

The authorship stops at the Focus tab. Calendar is a stock month grid plus agenda. Habits (week-dot rows, swatch-filled done circles, tinted streak pills) and Goals (title, percentage, progress bar) are the category default and speak a second visual language. Swatches tint surfaces, section labels drop to 13pt, cards float individually, and weights reach w800. The biggest missed opportunity is that habits and goals never use heat. A goal running past its target or a streak about to break is exactly the "nothing quietly rots" story, and those tabs tell it in generic dashboard terms.

**Deterministic scan**: not applicable (native platform).

## Overall Impression

Focus is the strongest screen in the app and it earns its metaphor. Around it, the system leaks:
- Red means two different things.
- Habits and Goals were not brought into the ticket world.
- On iOS, the user's real device, the native chrome is a single layer deep. The tab bar and large titles are right, but one tap in you get Material sheets and pickers and an editor you cannot swipe away from.

The single biggest opportunity is to make the first tap after the root as native and as on-brand as the root itself.

## What's Working

- **The pass ticket is a real hierarchy decision, not a card.** The single most-neglected task is printed large with Done (ink fill) and Later (outline) side by side. Deferral is a first-class, honest action instead of a guilt-free dismiss. The "Put off 3 times" line, three punch holes and red stamp all agree, so the escalation reads in under a second.
- **Colour discipline on Focus.** Priority is set in ink with a filled versus outlined flag. Heat (amber → thermal red) is the only hue, so "1d late" in amber and "4d late" in red are unmissable without shouting. Identity swatches are 8px dots, never fills. It holds in both themes, and the tokens are contrast-tested.
- **Adaptive root chrome that is actually adaptive.** On iOS: large collapsing titles, a translucent tab bar, the add action in the nav bar and a CupertinoSlidingSegmentedControl. On Android: a pinned top app bar, Material NavigationBar, an ink FAB and SegmentedButton. One visual world, two correct shells.

## Priority Issues

**[P1] The task editor disables the iOS edge-swipe back**
- **Why it matters**: `PopScope(canPop: false)` wraps the whole editor (task_editor_screen.dart:448-452). Every row tap on Focus, Calendar and Search opens this screen. On iPhone, the primary device, the left-edge back swipe is dead for every task, not only for recurring ones that need the scope prompt. The user has to reach the top-left Material `Icons.arrow_back`. This is the most-felt HIG violation in the app.
- **Fix**: Let the route pop (`canPop: true`), persist in `onPopInvokedWithResult`, and set `canPop: false` only while `_seriesContentChanged` on an occurrence actually needs the scope sheet. On iOS, use the platform back affordance (`BackButton`/`CupertinoNavigationBarBackButton`), not a hard-coded `Icons.arrow_back`.
- **Suggested command**: /impeccable adapt

**[P1] Completion feedback and Undo are fragile and invisible to assistive tech**
- **Why it matters**: Completing is the app's core moment, and its only recovery is "Undo" in an overlay toast (common.dart:407-563). The toast has these problems:
  - It is pinned to the top of the screen, out of thumb reach and over the nav bar.
  - It auto-dismisses after 4s.
  - Undo is a `GestureDetector` on 13pt text, so the target is about 16pt tall and has no button semantics.
  - There is no `SemanticsService.announce` or live region anywhere in lib/, so VoiceOver and TalkBack users get no confirmation at all.
  - On Android it replaces the platform snackbar position.

  The same overlay carries the 3-line "notifications are disabled…" message, which no one can read in 4s.
- **Fix**: Anchor the confirmation bottom-up, above the tab bar or FAB. Make Undo a real `TextButton` of at least 44pt (iOS) / 48dp (Android). Announce the message and mark it as a live region. Extend the duration when accessibility features are on or the text is long. Add Undo to "Moved to …" (defer increments the escalation counter and currently cannot be reversed).
- **Suggested command**: /impeccable harden

**[P1] Red means "critical" on Focus and "high priority" on Calendar**
- **Why it matters**: calendar_screen.dart:323-324 paints a day's dot `colors.overdue` when `hasHighPriority`. In the captures, Oct 28 (the high-priority proposal, 1d late, amber on Focus) gets a red dot. Oct 25 (the passport, 4d late, put off 3 times, thermal red on Focus) gets a plain ink dot. The calendar inverts the app's one colour rule exactly where the user goes to see what is behind, and DESIGN.md's "Priority Speaks Ink" rule is broken in shipped code.
- **Fix**: Colour calendar markers by `TicketAge.heat` (past days holding open tasks show heat, today and future days show ink). Never use colour for priority; if priority must show, use a filled versus hollow dot. Update the semantics label from "has high priority" to "1 late".
- **Suggested command**: /impeccable colorize

**[P2] Habits and Goals don't belong to the ticket world**
- **Why it matters**: Habits:
  - Done days fill with the swatch colour (habits_screen.dart:883).
  - The checkpoint/timer panel is a swatch tint at alpha .09 with swatch-coloured w700 text (779-801).
  - Streak pills are swatch tints at alpha .12 in w800 (976-997).
  - Section labels are a private 13pt `_SectionLabel` while Focus and Goals use 15pt `SectionHeader`.
  - Cards float with 12px gaps instead of one rail strip.
  - Spacing literals (6, 9, 10, 13, 14, 18) sit off the 4/8 rhythm.

  Every one of these is a "Don't" in DESIGN.md. The app reads as Focus plus three borrowed screens, and a swatch starts to carry meaning ("done", "today"), which the Identity Dot Rule forbids.
- **Fix**: Done = an ink-filled circle with a paper check. The swatch stays as the 8–10px title dot only. Replace the pills and tinted panels with ink `MetaChip`s, and use `SectionHeader` plus `RailTile` strips. Bring a goal past its target or a broken streak into the heat ramp so these tabs tell the product's story too.
- **Suggested command**: /impeccable polish

**[P2] iOS fidelity collapses one tap in**
- **Why it matters**: The root shell adapts, but most secondary surfaces do not:
  - Defer, snooze and series-scope pickers are Material `showModalBottomSheet` + `ListTile`s with Material icons (`wb_sunny`, `next_week`, `looks_one`, `all_inclusive`) (task_flows.dart).
  - Dates and times use Material `showDatePicker`/`showTimePicker`.
  - Goal detail uses a Material `PopupMenuButton` and `Icons.tune_rounded`.
  - The notification-permission explainer, a spec-critical moment, is a plain Material `AlertDialog` (task_flows.dart:230), unlike every other dialog, which is `.adaptive`.

  In total there are about 33 Material-only modal call sites against adaptive handling at the root. A fluent iPhone user hits a web-like or Android-like surface on the second tap of the core defer flow.
- **Fix**: Use `CupertinoActionSheet` for defer, snooze and scope on iOS, `CupertinoDatePicker` in a sheet for dates and times, `showAdaptiveDialog` + `dialogAction` for the permission explainer, and a pull-down/context menu instead of `PopupMenuButton`. Route all of these through `isCupertino(context)` the way `_FocusMenu` already does.
- **Suggested command**: /impeccable adapt

## Persona Red Flags

**Casey (Distracted Mobile User)**, one-handed between other things, which matches PRODUCT.md's stated context:
- After tapping a row's circle, Undo appears at the very top of the screen for 4s, the furthest point from the thumb.
- After opening a task to glance at it, the edge-swipe back doesn't work on iPhone. Casey has to reach the top-left back arrow.
- To push a rail task to tomorrow, Casey has to know the swipe-left gesture. Only the pass ticket has a visible Later button.
- Quick add: Casey taps "Reminder" without picking a date, saves, and gets no reminder and no message (quick_add_sheet.dart:96 only applies reminders when `dueDate != null`). This bends Principle 4, "Honest about the system".

**Sam (Accessibility-Dependent)**:
- Completing a task produces no spoken confirmation. The toast is an overlay with no announcement, and its Undo is a bare `GestureDetector` with no button role.
- Habit day buttons read "Mark 2026-10-23 complete for [demo] Morning walk on 2026-10-23". That is an ISO date, spoken twice, because `DateOnly.toString()` is used for both the action and the label (habits_screen.dart:850-857). The tooltip shows that ISO string to sighted users too.
- Fixed-height containers won't grow with large text: calendar cells (46px), habit day circles (30px with a caption numeral inside) and the 44px row checkbox box. Font scale and Dynamic Type are unverified (PRODUCT.md says so), and rail body text is 15pt against iOS's 17pt Body.

**Jordan (Confused First-Timer)**, relevant because a public store release is planned:
- A grey hollow ring under "Sunday" on "[demo] Pay electricity bill" is a cold punch mark. It sits in the same row as the hollow completion circle and looks like a second checkbox or radio button. Nothing tells Jordan what punch marks or the amber/red heat mean.
- The flag icon means both the Goals tab and High priority (a filled flag on the selected Goals tab and on a High task).
- "Focus" names a tab, a timer and, on iPhone, a system feature.

## Minor Observations

- **The exit sequence leaves the paper behind.** `_Spike` wraps the row inside `RailTile`, so the text slides off a blank white slot rather than the ticket leaving the rail. At 2.47s the checked circle is still visible at the right edge. DESIGN.md promises the ticket "slides fully off".
- The editor's subtask progress bar is `colors.done` green (task_editor_screen.dart:517). Every other progress bar (row, habits summary, goal card) is ink.
- w800 appears in habits_screen.dart (4×) and habit_detail_screen.dart (5×). DESIGN.md's ramp tops out at 700.
- The Calendar's first section header sits 4px under the grid divider and looks cramped next to the 24px rhythm elsewhere.
- On iOS the Calendar nav bar can carry four trailing items (Today, ‹, ›, +) over a large title. The chevrons are Material `Icons.chevron_left/right`, not CupertinoIcons.
- The Quick add "Detected:" line uses an `auto_awesome` sparkle in accent, an AI-sparkle idiom foreign to the ink/heat world.
- The priority chip in Quick add always shows a filled flag and inks itself when "Low" is selected, so Low looks as loud as a selected "Today".
- The task editor stacks 10 sections at once: Schedule, Reminders, Subtasks, Details, Priority, Tags, Progress, Focus timer, Appearance, footer. Progressive disclosure would cut the scroll in half.
- Every habit card subtitle reads "Daily check-in", repeating the Daily segment above it.
- Goals puts "1 in progress, 0 done" and "Your goals" above a single card: two label tiers for one item.
- The Habits storage warning says "Please try again" but offers nothing to tap.
- Tapping the active tab doesn't scroll to top on iOS (`_select` only sets the index).
- **Cognitive load: moderate, 3 of 8 checklist items fail.** Chunking and progressive disclosure fail in the editor. Minimal choices fails in Quick add, which shows 5 chips and 2 buttons at once, so 7 options.
- **Emotional journey**: the peak is the strike-and-slide spike with haptics, which is good. The end is a small top toast that vanishes. The first thing seen on launch is a 34pt red "4d" with "Put off 3 times" in red; that is pressure, and it needs a reassuring way out to stay "without punishment".

## Questions to Consider

- What would Habits look like if a streak about to break warmed up exactly like a ticket?
- If deferral is half the product, why is it a button on one ticket and a secret gesture on all the others?
- Should the calendar show where the heat is (which days hold late tickets) rather than where the priority is?
- What does the moment after "Done" feel like for someone who can't see the top of the screen?
- Does the editor need to show Focus timer, Progress and Appearance before the user has asked for them?
